## 0. Bottom line

Spotify has not grown its music margin by beating the labels on price. The filings show two label-granted concessions doing the work: (1) a royalty percentage that is lower only while Spotify meets contractual targets on subscribers, the ratio of free users to subscribers, and subscriber churn, and (2) the Marketplace rebate, in which labels and artists accept a lower royalty (Discovery Mode charges a 30% commission on recording royalties) in return for algorithmic promotion. Both are contingent. The first is exposed to the volume and churn damage the pricing pillar predicts, and it moves in steps rather than a slope; the second is the Street's main non-price source of gross-margin expansion (Deutsche Bank models 65-70bp a year) and exists at the labels' discretion. Neither the target structure nor Discovery Mode appears in any of the 83 sell-side notes.

## 1. How Spotify grew margin and held churn inside a label oligopoly

### 1.1 The royalty rate is earned, not fixed

FY25 20-F, Item 5 (Components of our Operating Results): *"In our agreements with certain record labels, the percentage of revenue used in the calculation of royalties is generally dependent upon certain targets being met. The targets can include such measures as the number of applicable Premium Subscribers, the ratio of Ad-Supported Users to applicable Premium Subscribers, and/or the rates of applicable Premium Subscriber churn."*

- The same sentence is in every 20-F in the folder (FY21 to FY25). The word "applicable" first appears in the 20-F filed Feb 5, 2025, days after the UMG renewal (Jan 26, 2025). What it narrows is not disclosed.
- The auditor's critical audit matter on music royalty costs lists the "ratio of Ad-Supported Users to applicable Premium Subscribers" among the variables tested, so the ratio is a live input to the royalty calculation, not boilerplate.
- Recognition (Note 2g): *"In certain contracts, payments to rights holders can be due based on uncertain future events which might not be resolved for several months. Where this is the case, the Group recognizes this expense only if and when the uncertainty is resolved."*
- The only public sizing of a tier: the 2017 UMG deal let Spotify cut royalties on Universal's recordings "to about 52 percent, from about 55 percent," a change "contingent on Spotify reaching certain subscriber goals over the term of the contract" (CNBC, Apr 5, 2017, citing people briefed on the terms). Music Business Worldwide (Jun 2017) described Spotify as "pegged to subscriber growth targets."

This explains management's behaviour. Spotify's cost of goods rewards it for keeping churn low, growing subscribers and limiting free users relative to payers. It runs three trial programs a year (Q2, Q3 and Q4 since 2022, per the 20-F), pushes Family and Duo, calls retention "best-in-class", and in Q2'26 "carefully introduced some friction in both ad load and some limitations in our free tier" in emerging markets. The Street reads the last move as pulling the "monetization lever." The contract gives a second reading: the ratio of Ad-Supported MAUs to Premium subscribers, a royalty variable, is at a record.

### 1.2 Marketplace did most of the music-margin work

- Management (Investor Day, May 21, 2026): gross margin went from 25% in 2022 to 32% in 2025 and "about 1/3 of that came from the music business," so roughly 2.3pp. (FY22 gross profit €2,926m on €11,727m of revenue = 25.0%; FY25 €5,496m on €17,186m = 32.0%.)
- Morgan Stanley (Aug 3, 2026) estimates the consolidated Marketplace benefit at 196bp in 2022 and 375bp in 2025 (+179bp), and says "the vast majority" of the music improvement came from it. Its series: 38bp (2018), 47, 81, 167, 196, 247, 294, 375bp (2025).
- Spotify's own 20-F names marketplace programs as a driver of Premium gross margin in FY21, FY23, FY24 and FY25. In FY23, a year with a US price increase (July), the stated drivers of the one-point Premium GM gain were "benefits from certain marketplace programs and reduced streaming delivery costs."

### 1.3 Pricing, net of the labels, contributed little over the cycle

About 2.3pp of music contribution less about 1.8pp of Marketplace leaves roughly 0.5pp over three years that included US increases in July 2023 and June 2024 and price rises in 150+ markets in 2025. This is approximate (the "one third" is management's rounding and the Marketplace series is an MS estimate), but the direction supports the lap argument without leaning on escalator language: over a full cycle the labels kept most of Spotify's price increases, and the music margin that did appear came from the rebate.

### 1.4 Why churn stays low

Two label-driven reasons sit alongside the product. First, the floors are industry-wide: Warner has PSM increases across "all of our major DSP partners," and DSP list prices move together (JPM, Sep 15 UMG note, Figures 9-10; Apple raised in July 2026; Norström on the Q2 call: "happy to be the price leader"). No full-catalogue competitor can undercut Spotify on price, so churn to rivals stays low. Second, the contract pays Spotify to retain, so Spotify spends on retention, and part of that spend is itself a payment to the labels: the 20-F says the cost of free trials is *"typically per user royalty fees, determined in accordance with the rights holder agreements,"* booked in sales and marketing, while discounted trials sit in cost of revenue.

What would break the first: a service where music is a bundled extra (JPM estimates only about half of YouTube Premium subscribers actively use YouTube Music, and YouTube Premium sells at roughly a 30% discount to the standalone parts). What would break the second: the pricing pillar's elasticity showing up in churn and gross adds.

## 3. Variant view 1: the tiered rate is a margin cliff attached to the pricing pillar

**Street view.** The royalty rate is a smooth percentage of revenue; churn and net adds are volume questions (e.g. Morgan Stanley's Aug 3 card-panel churn work). No note or transcript links churn, subscriber growth or the free-to-paid ratio to the royalty rate.

**Our view.** If the pricing pillar is right (elasticity after the January 2026 US increase, a flat US paying base, lower-quality emerging-market adds), the damage does not stop at revenue. Missing a subscriber, ratio or churn target resets the percentage of revenue paid to that label, and under the recognition policy above it would land as a discrete cost-of-revenue hit in the quarter the uncertainty resolves, not as a gradual drift that consensus could model. That is the link that turns the two pillars into one argument: if the pricing pillar is right, the margin pillar follows through the contract.

**Size (illustrative; thresholds and steps are undisclosed).** Each 1pp of Premium revenue is about €200m on FY27 consensus Premium revenue of €20.2bn (MODL quarterly sum), about 90bp of FY27 group gross margin (€22.4bn revenue) and about €0.75 of EPS (about 5% of FY27 consensus €15.41). The 2017 precedent was a 3-point step on one major's recordings.

**Evidence for.** Primary-source mechanism; the ratio is at a record 1.65 with free users growing faster than subscribers in 1H26; management acted on free-tier growth in Q2; Q3 net adds are guided to 5M from 7M in Q2; UMG's ex-Downtown subscription streaming grew 6.7% in Q2, 3.5pp of it price (JPM), and Bazinet's Q2 question cited label data showing industry premium growth slowed; US card-panel customers +0.5% y/y Q3-to-date.

**Evidence against.** Morgan Stanley's card data (Aug 3) shows a smaller churn bump after the February 2026 increase, and Evercore's survey (Jul 19) has churn intent at a record low; Spotify says retention is best-in-class; the 2025 renewals may have reset targets to levels Spotify clears easily; we cannot see thresholds, measurement periods or step sizes.

**FE framing.** Module 12: "some perception risk that is not currently being discussed, but could increase as a 'hard to disprove' controversy in the future." It also answers the judge's question "why hasn't the oligopoly crushed the margin?" with a mechanism rather than an assertion.

## 4. Variant view 2: Marketplace is the Street's non-price margin engine, and it is a label concession

**What is priced in.** Deutsche Bank (May 22) "continue[s] to estimate marketplace can drive 65-70bps of annual gross margin expansion"; Morgan Stanley attributes most past music-margin gains to it; the CFO listed marketplace among the 2026 drivers on the Q4'25 call. From about €645m of 2025 gross profit (JPM; MS ~€650m), 65-70bp a year requires marketplace gross profit to grow about 30% a year on consensus revenue (+34% FY26, +32% FY27, +28% FY28), reaching roughly €1.4-1.5bn by FY28, about 2.3x FY25. Each 10 points of growth shortfall costs about 35bp of gross margin a year at the current size. Consensus total GM expansion is +100bp in FY27, so marketplace at the DB pace would be about two-thirds of it.

**What it is.** 20-F: *"voluntary marketplace programs ... Some of these programs apply a discounted royalty rate to streams generated in specified recommendation contexts,"* while *"certain offerings within our two-sided marketplace result in advertising revenues."* Discovery Mode charges *"a 30% commission is applied to recording royalties generated from all streams of selected songs ... in Discovery Mode contexts"* (Spotify for Artists), described as Radio, Autoplay and certain Mixes (Rolling Stone, Nov 2025). It was built through label negotiations (Ek, Q1'20 call, on the 2020 renewals: "the focus for these renewals were really around establishing the marketplace") and, per former CFO Vogel (Q3'23 call), "Our marketplace only works if it's working for the labels."

**Why it could come in below expectations.**
1. It is a relative-visibility product in a pro-rata pool. Each enrolled track gains share from others, so as enrollment rises the lift per track falls while the 30% discount still applies to every enrolled stream. The Street's path takes it to about 2.3x its 2025 size inside the same lean-back inventory.
2. The labels' incentives have turned. JPM (Jul 31) says UMG's Q2 "amplified concerns regarding UMG's negotiating power with DSPs and its ability to secure price rises." A 30% commission lowers the label's revenue per stream, the opposite of the Streaming 2.0 metric (wholesale pricing contribution) investors now judge labels on.
3. Spotify's roadmap moves lean-back listening toward AI surfaces (DJ, Prompted Playlists). Whether they are Discovery Mode contexts is not stated in the sources we could read; if they are not, eligible inventory shrinks, and if they are, the disclosure question gets harder.
4. Part of marketplace is booked as advertising revenue, inside an ad business growing 3%.

Not a catalyst: the "payola" class action (filed Nov 5, 2025, S.D.N.Y.) was compelled to individual arbitration in May 2026.

**Evidence against.** Growth of about 40% a year in 2021-25 with no visible slowdown (MS's 2025 increment of +81bp is its largest); labels opt in because it pays for them individually; the commission scales with royalties per stream, so it partly hedges label price increases.

## 5. Variant view 3: ad-tier floors against a consensus that expects Ad-Supported GM to jump

- **Consensus (MODL, revenue less cost of revenue):** Ad-Supported GM 13.0% in Q1'26 to 17.8% in Q1'27E (Bloomberg's GM field shows 17.3%); 18.9% in FY26E to 21.3% in FY27E. JPM and MS attribute the Q2'26 gain to "favorable podcast & tax," not music.
- **Contract terms (20-F):** ad-tier royalties are typically a percentage of revenue, but *"certain agreements are based on the greater of a percentage of relevant revenue and an amount for each time a track is streamed."* Label deals can require *"advertising inventory at discounted rates"*, and rights holders receive non-transferable advertising credits for which no revenue is recognized.
- **Why it matters now:** with ad revenue +3% in Q1 and Q2'26 and Ad-Supported MAUs +14%, revenue per free stream is falling, which is when per-stream floors bind and when free inventory handed to labels costs the most.
- **Size:** if Ad-Supported GM holds at FY26 levels, about 40bp off Q1'27 group GM (€21m) and about 23bp off FY27 (€52m).
- **Counter:** emerging-market free-tier friction cuts the lowest-yield streams, which is what management means by music margins improving "particularly in emerging markets" (a line from the Ad-Supported section of the Investor Day remarks).

## 6. Smaller items

- **Estimate mechanics.** 20-F: "From time to time, we accrue royalties based on management estimates of the rates that will apply while we negotiate license agreement renewals." The Q3'25 beat included €11m (Premium) plus part of €17m (Ad-Supported) of prior-period estimate changes "nearly all related to the first half of 2025," in the quarter Sony re-signed (Sept 18, 2025); a target resolution would also fit. Deals carrying minimum guarantees are "frequently between one and three years," so the next negotiations may overlap the 1H27 lap. Direction is unknowable; it argues against trusting a single-quarter GM beat. Aged rights-holder liabilities rose to €406m at end-FY25 from €351m.
- **MFN.** The 20-F accrues additional royalties when another licensor gets better terms. Near-term relevance is the AI tier (UMG deal at Investor Day, Merlin in Q2, "a possible Spotify AI-rights agreement" in WMG's fiscal Q4 per Deutsche Bank).
- **For the opex pillar.** Free-trial royalty fees sit in sales and marketing, so part of the label take is in opex, and a shift from free to discounted trials moves it into cost of revenue.

## 10. Thesis v2 (Sept 26): built on KPIs and behaviour, not contract terms

Joel's steer: avoid theses that depend on contract terms the buy-side may know better; focus on what drives the KPIs the contracts reference.

**Thesis in two sentences.** Three US price increases in 30 months have slowed Spotify's highest-value subscriber growth, so Spotify is now buying growth through a more generous free tier (Sept 15, 2025), trials and emerging markets, and its label contracts charge for each: ad-tier music costs rise with free listening, free-trial users carry per-user royalty fees, and the 20-F ties Premium royalty rates to subscriber growth, the free-to-paid ratio and churn (free users per payer now at a record 1.65). The Street treats these costs as a short-term conversion lag and models ~100bp of FY27 gross-margin expansion with opex fading, missing that once the January 2026 price increase laps in 1H27 there is no price step left to absorb them.

**What is not priced.** (1) The link from those KPIs to the Premium royalty rate (20-F; 0 of 83 notes). **Honest scorecard (Sept 26).** Causing factor: specific, dated, observable. What the Street misses: real but narrower than "nobody noticed" (the cost is known; its persistence and the rate link are not). Magnitude: weak. ### 10.1 Revision (Sept 26, later): free-tier friction and the two-sentence thesis

**Friction.** Since the Q2'26 call (Aug 4, 2026) Spotify has added friction to the free tier in select emerging markets (India, Indonesia): higher ad load, sign-up changes and email verification, dropped support for low-end Android devices, and limits on free features, alongside AI-personalized ad load. It is widely covered (Barclays and others) and priced as a positive ("monetization lever"). The September 2025 upgrade stays in place outside those markets. Consequence: the ad-tier cost leg is weak (more ad load raises revenue per free user, so the consensus Ad-Supported GM recovery is plausible); demote it to a footnote. The friction supports the rate leg as revealed preference (Spotify gave up MAU growth while promising double-digit ad growth) and moves the test to subscribers, because friction only helps if it converts.

**Thesis (two sentences).** Spotify pays the labels a lower royalty rate only while it keeps growing subscribers and converting free users, and both are slowing after three US price increases (subscribers +12% to +9% y/y; free users +14%). The Street models FY27 gross margin up ~100bp as if that rate were fixed, so a subscriber slowdown would hit margin, not just revenue.

## 11. Gameplan (Sept 26): prove the free:premium ratio moves the payout rate

**Metric choice: free users per Premium subscriber.** Quarterly history since the F-1 (2015-17) and every 6-K since; the auditor's critical audit matter names it as a royalty variable; it moved 1.57 to 1.65 in four quarters; management is acting on it. Subscriber growth is the backup (same data). Churn has not been disclosed numerically since the listing, so it can only be proxied (Second Measure retention).

**Workstream 1: data panel.** Spotify quarterly KPIs and segment revenue/cost of revenue 2015-2Q26 (F-1, 20-Fs, 6-Ks, decks; note the Jan 2026 segment reclass). 20-F royalty narrative by year (rate increases, marketplace, "changes in prior period estimates" amounts and sign, aged rights-holder liabilities, MG commitments). Loud & Clear annual payouts. Controls: price-step dates, US ATV (card panel), renewal dates (2017, 2019-20, 2025), bundle (Mar 2024), podcast restructuring, marketplace. Label side: WMG 10-K and UMG annual report customer-concentration disclosures (check each year), Bloomberg SPLC (flag estimated vs disclosed), 20-F majors+Merlin share of streams by year, recorded vs publishing split.

**Workstream 2: separate the two negatives.** Structural channel (more free users) lives in the Ad-Supported segment and in consolidated mix. Contract channel lives in Premium's royalty ratio, because Premium revenue does not change when free users grow and royalties are booked by segment at segment-specific rates. Test within Premium, not consolidated GM. Unallocated costs are split by user activity, so a rising free share pushes cost out of Premium: a bias against us (conservative). Signatures differ: structural = continuous and contemporaneous; contract = stepwise, lagged, lumpy, aligned to measurement periods and renewals, and visible in prior-period estimate true-ups. Tests: (a) y/y Premium GM (or music cost ratio) on ARPU/ATV, price-step dummies and known items, then add the ratio (levels and lags 1-4Q) and check the incremental fit; (b) piecewise/threshold check for a break in the Premium royalty ratio; (c) true-up fingerprint: map every disclosed estimate change to the KPI path in its measurement window. Naive correlation over the last year runs the wrong way (ratio up, Premium GM up on price), so controls are essential.

**Workstream 3: label triangulation.** Implied payout rate for label L = L's revenue from Spotify / (Spotify revenue x L's share of Spotify streams). Growth-rate form avoids needing the level: change in implied rate = change in L's Spotify revenue - change in Spotify revenue - change in L's stream share (in logs, FX-adjusted, recorded music only). WMG is the most likely to have usable disclosure. Cross-check with the aggregate rate (Loud & Clear payouts / revenue). Correlate with the ratio and subscriber growth by year; expect weak power (8-10 annual points), so look for discontinuities rather than correlations, and read label calls for "catch-up" or true-up language in the same periods.

**Workstream 4: back into the rate and materiality.** Convert any coefficient into bp of Premium royalty per 0.05 of ratio; apply to consensus (ratio ~1.65 flat) and our path; compare with the 2017 ~3pp precedent.

**Kill test.** If, after controls, the ratio adds no explanatory power for Premium's royalty ratio and the true-ups show no pattern, the thesis goes back to a tail risk and the pitch leads with the lap.

## 12. Test results: free-to-paid ratio vs label payouts (Sept 27, 2026)

Full report: `SPOT_FreeToPaid_LabelPayout_Analysis.pdf` (this folder). Data, figures and scripts: `FreeToPaid_Analysis/`.

**Verdict.** The mechanism is documented and the history points the right way, but the correlation does not survive controls. By the rule set in section 11 this is the "demote" outcome unless expert calls confirm the targets are still live after the 2025 renewals.

**Payment structure.** Labels are paid on their own catalogue's stream share of a per-market, per-tier royalty pool (Söderström, Investor Day, Jun 8, 2022), at the greater of a headline % of revenue, a per-subscriber minimum, or a per-play rate (Muir, UMG, Nov 20, 2024; WMG describes two prongs). They do not take a share of all ARPU. Discounts and trials raise the effective rate where the minimum binds; price increases do the reverse.

**Spotify side (2022Q1 to 2026Q2, n = 18).** Premium GM y/y vs ratio y/y: r = -0.46 (p = 0.054). Price-only control: -81bp per +0.1 of ratio (p = 0.10), R-squared 0.50 to 0.59. Adding Car Thing and bundle dummies: -22bp (lag 0), -43bp (lag 1), -44bp (lag 2), p = 0.17 to 0.52. Negative in 9 of 10 specifications. Subscriber growth adds nothing (p = 0.34). Churn untestable. Annual 2020-2025: r = -0.68 (p = 0.14, n = 6).

**Volume, price, rate (EUR m, sum of y/y changes in Premium cost of revenue).** Total 4,397 = volume 4,386 + price 791 + rate -781. ## 13. Drill plan (Sept 27, later): why section 12's null may be low-power, and the tests that would settle it

Section 12 already controlled for price, so "normalize for the price spikes" does not rescue the result. The case for continuing rests on three design problems with that test, not on the data looking better once adjusted.

1. **Wrong tool for a step.** Targets reset the rate in discrete steps, recognized only when the uncertainty resolves (Note 2g). A y/y regression over 18 quarters is built to find a slope. Calibration test: extend the panel to 2015 and check whether the same method detects the 2017 UMG step (~55% to ~52%), which we know happened. If it cannot, the 2022-26 null says little.
2. **Wrong baseline.** Targets are set at renewal (UMG Jan 2025, WMG Feb 2025, Sony Sept 2025), presumably off Spotify's plan at the time. The relevant question is deviation from that baseline (subs about +12% y/y, ratio about 1.57 at the UMG renewal), not absolute levels. Since then: subs +9%, ratio 1.65. The 2022-26 sample mixes pre- and post-renewal contracts.
3. **Possibly wrong metric.** "Applicable" was added in the 20-F filed days after the UMG renewal. If it scopes targets to specific markets or plans, the consolidated ratio is the wrong input. Build a regional proxy from 6-K regional MAU and subscriber splits ((MAU - subs) / subs for North America, Europe, EM). Caveat: the ratio rise is mostly EM-driven, so if targets are developed-market only, the ratio leg weakens and the subscriber-growth and churn legs carry the thesis.

**Tell and timing.** If measurement periods are annual, a 2026 miss lands as an unfavourable "changes in prior period estimates" line in Q4'26 or Q1'27, the same window as the January 2026 price lap.

**Standing counter-evidence.** 2024 rate term -EUR 511m with the ratio flat; 1H26 rate term -EUR 135m while the ratio rose 0.07-0.08.

**Pitch role until tests 1-3 or an expert call confirm.** The mechanism that links the pricing pillar to margin, not a sized leg.

## 14. Forward leg (Sept 27): consensus needs free-user growth to halve for the ratio to stay flat

Joel's steer: pair the rate mechanism with a forward view that the free-to-paid ratio does not stay flat. Sections 12-13 test whether the ratio moves the rate (mechanism); this section is the forecast that would trigger it. Both are needed: a rising ratio matters to margin only through the mechanism, and the mechanism matters only if the ratio moves.

**Consensus path (Bloomberg MODL, reported Ad-Supported users / Premium subscribers, segment rows):**

| Quarter | Free users | y/y | Subs | y/y | Free per payer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q2'26A | 494 | +14% | 300 | +9% | 1.65 |
| Q3'26E | 498 | +12% | 305 | +9% | 1.63 |
| Q4'26E | 520 | +9% | 315 | +9% | 1.65 |
| Q1'27E | 524 | +9% | 318 | +9% | 1.65 |
| Q2'27E | 534 | +8% | 325 | +8% | 1.64 |
| Q4'27E | 561 | +8% | 339 | +8% | 1.65 |

**What that assumes.** Free-user growth falls from +14% to +8% within four quarters while subscriber growth holds at 8-9%. The ratio is flat only because the two growth rates converge.

**Sensitivity at Q2'27 (subs at consensus 325m).** Free growth +11%: ratio 1.69. Free growth held at +14%: ratio 1.73. To keep 1.65 with free growth at +11%, subscribers must grow about 11%, above the current 9%.

**Variant.** The pricing pillar says subscriber growth slows further as the January 2026 increase laps and the US/EU base saturates. Spotify then has two outcomes, both worse than consensus: (a) it lets free users keep growing and the ratio rises past 1.65, or (b) it holds the ratio with more free-tier friction and MAU growth undershoots consensus (+8-9%), hitting the ad-growth story. The Q2'26 friction is evidence Spotify already manages to (b).

**Counter.** Friction may convert free users rather than lose them (ratio falls, subs beat); Q3'26 is the first print with friction in effect.

**Q3'26 pre-registration.** Consensus: free 498m, subs 305m, ratio 1.63. Free above ~505m with subs at or below consensus supports (a); free below ~490m with subs at or below consensus supports (b).
